# Research trail

This directory is the publication's canonical evidence and provenance package.
It travels with the publication from Desk to Shelf as part of a deliberate
release snapshot.

Use it to leave durable context for the author, readers, reviewers, researchers,
and future agents without turning the manuscript into a research notebook.

## Source ledger

Record material sources here or link to focused notes in this directory. For
each important source, capture enough to recover why it mattered:

- author / organization and title;
- publication or revision date when known;
- URL plus access date for changing web sources;
- DOI, report number, dataset version, or other stable identifier when useful;
- manuscript chapter, passage, claim, figure, or calculation informed;
- the relevant observation in your own words;
- limitations, assumptions, uncertainty, counterevidence, or competing views;
- transformations or calculations for derived numbers;
- `recheck before release` when a claim can age quickly.

Separate what a source establishes from what the manuscript infers. Prefer
primary and authoritative evidence when it answers the question, but preserve
useful secondary synthesis and disagreement too.

## Reader-facing evidence

Promote evidence a reader needs while reading into manuscript citations,
footnotes, references, figures, methodology, or back matter. Keep the deeper
claim checks, source ledger, calculations, counterevidence, and release notes
here.

`research/` is publication content but is not automatically part of the Reader
table of contents.

## Source-material rights

A research trail is provenance, not permission to republish its sources. Prefer
links, bibliographic metadata, lawful short quotations, hashes, and your own
notes over copied third-party files. Commit source PDFs, datasets, images, or
other third-party artifacts only when redistribution is clearly authorized, and
preserve their provenance/license information.

Never put secrets, credentials, confidential material, or personal data that
should not be public here. A public Desk makes committed research public even if
the Desk is lower-profile than the Shelf.

## Before release

- Recheck time-sensitive material.
- Preserve material caveats and counterevidence.
- Promote reader-relevant evidence into the manuscript where appropriate.
- Make calculations and derived claims reproducible enough for their importance.
- Confirm any third-party files are redistributable.
- Confirm everything in this directory is appropriate for the intended Shelf
  release.

See `docs/research.md` in Bookself upstream for the full research contract.
