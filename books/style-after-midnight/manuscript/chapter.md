# The last train

At 12:17 the platform clock changed without making a sound.

Jonah noticed because there was nothing else left to watch. The kiosk had closed. The departure board showed only one line. Beyond the tracks, apartment windows floated in the dark like pages from different books, each lit for reasons he would never know.

The last train was three minutes late.

He put his phone away and let his eyes adjust. The station lights were brighter than the night but somehow less comforting. They flattened everything: bench, sign, yellow line, the silver rails disappearing into the tunnel.

A woman at the far end of the platform turned a page in a paperback.

Jonah could not see the title. He could see the small movement of the paper and the way she leaned toward it, as if the book made its own weather around her.

Then the tunnel breathed.

The sound arrived first, low and mechanical. A pale rectangle appeared in the distance, followed by the two white points of the train. Everyone on the platform stood a little straighter.

For the length of a breath, Jonah wished it would not arrive.

There was something complete about waiting when nothing more could be done. No message to send. No route to compare. No earlier train to catch. Only the dark track, the warm book in a stranger's hands, and the certainty that eventually a door would open.

The train entered the station with a rush of reflected light.
