# The unlocked door

The hallway light on the fourth floor had burned out again.

Mina knew this before the elevator opened because darkness arrived first: a narrow black seam between the doors, widening until the emergency lamp inside the car spilled a weak rectangle across the carpet.

Apartment 4C was at the end of the hall.

Her apartment.

She stepped out and heard the elevator close behind her.

For a moment nothing moved.

Then the chain on 4B clicked.

“Ms. Vale?” Mr. Han called through his door.

“Yes?”

“You have a visitor.”

Mina stopped.

“What visitor?”

The chain stayed taut. “Young man. Gray coat. He waited maybe twenty minutes.”

“I wasn't expecting anyone.”

“That is what I told him.”

The door closed gently.

Mina took out her phone and turned on the flashlight. The beam found the framed print above the radiator, the brass number on 4C, the small scrape near the lock where she always missed with the key when carrying groceries.

The door was open by half an inch.

She did not touch it.

Every sensible instruction she had ever heard arrived at once: go downstairs, call the police, do not enter, do not announce yourself. Under those instructions was another thought, quieter and less useful.

The cat.

“Juniper?” she whispered.

No bell. No soft complaint from inside.

Mina backed toward the elevator and pressed the button. Nothing happened. Of course. The car had already gone down.

From inside 4C came a sound like paper sliding across paper.

She froze.

Then a voice said, “Mina?”

Not a stranger's voice.

That was worse.

She pushed the door with two fingers.

The living room was lit only by the streetlamp outside, its orange light striped by the blinds. A man sat at her kitchen table with both hands visible. Gray coat. Wet hair. A sealed envelope lay between them though there was no *them* yet, not until she stepped into the room.

He looked older than the photograph in her desk drawer and younger than the version she had spent twelve years inventing.

“You should not be here,” Mina said.

“I know.”

“Where is my cat?”

He blinked. “The cat is your first question?”

“Where.”

A soft thump came from the bedroom. Juniper appeared, stretched extravagantly, and regarded the visitor with the moral exhaustion of a creature forced to supervise adults.

Mina let out a breath.

The man touched the envelope but did not move it toward her.

“I found something after Mom died.”

Mina stayed by the door.

Rain ticked against the fire escape.

“You had twelve years to find my address,” she said.

“I had twelve years to be afraid of it.”

The hallway behind her remained dark. The apartment ahead of her was familiar in every detail except the person at the table.

Mina reached back and closed the door.

She did not lock it.
