# The useful edge

A margin is not empty space. It is a decision about what deserves room.

Modern reading interfaces often treat width as free. A browser can stretch from one side of a large display to the other, so the tempting choice is to let the text follow. That produces more words per line and fewer screens, but not necessarily better reading.

## Measure is a tool

A useful reading width does three jobs at once:

- it keeps the return from one line to the next easy to find;
- it gives headings and quotations a visible structure;
- it leaves enough surrounding quiet for the text to feel intentional.

The `modern-essay` preset is deliberately more open than Bookself's literary profiles. It uses a sans serif face, left alignment, and a wider measure because essays, reports, and web-native longform often benefit from an editorial rather than novelistic voice.

The result should feel crisp, not compressed.

## Scroll can be a design choice

Continuous reading is not merely a fallback for small screens. For an argument that moves through headings, examples, lists, and short sections, Scroll can preserve conceptual momentum. The reader sees the shape of the piece while moving through it.

That does not mean the author owns the mode.

If you prefer pages, open the Reading experience panel and choose **Pages**. If you prefer a serif face, choose one. Those changes belong to your browser and will not alter this publication for another reader.

The author chooses a starting composition. The reader chooses the conditions under which they can stay with it.

That boundary is part of the design, too.
