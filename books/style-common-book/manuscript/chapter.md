# A page that gets out of the way

Most books do not need to make a theatrical entrance. They need a page that feels composed, readable, and familiar enough that the reader can stop thinking about the interface.

The `book` preset is Bookself's neutral starting point for that job. It uses a bookish serif face, an ivory page, balanced line length, modest leading, and gentle first-line indentation. The result is intended to feel like a well-made trade book rather than a themed reading environment.

That neutrality is useful because not every publication wants a strong visual mood. A memoir, practical guide, history, biography, or general nonfiction title may need typography that supports many kinds of passages without calling attention to itself.

## Quiet structure

Headings establish hierarchy. Paragraphs establish pace. Lists, quotations, and links remain visually distinct without turning the page into a dashboard.

> A default is successful when it feels considered before it feels styled.

This is also the preset to copy when an author wants to start conservatively and then tune one or two values in `reader.json`: perhaps a slightly larger size, a narrower measure, or a different paper atmosphere.

The reader can still change any of those choices locally. The book recommends a composition; the browser belongs to the person reading it.
