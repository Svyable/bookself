# Clarity is a feature

Reading settings are not decoration.

For some readers, a larger type size is more comfortable. For others, left-aligned text makes the line easier to follow. Some people need stronger contrast. Some prefer more space between lines and paragraphs.

A good Reader treats these choices as normal.

## This example starts with clarity

The `accessible` preset recommends:

- Atkinson Hyperlegible;
- 22 pixel text;
- high contrast paper and ink;
- generous line spacing;
- a narrow line width;
- left alignment;
- no automatic hyphenation;
- continuous Scroll reading.

These are recommendations, not a definition of accessibility for every person.

You might read better with a dark high-contrast theme. You might prefer a serif typeface. You might need 28 pixel text. You might want Pages instead of Scroll.

Use the Reading experience controls to change any setting.

## Your changes are private to your browser

When you change the typeface or colors, Bookself marks those areas as your local preferences. A different reader opening the same publication does not inherit your settings.

The book stays the same in Git. Your browser keeps your reading environment.

If you want to see this publication's recommended design again, choose **Use this book's design**.

The important capability is not that one preset is “accessible.” The important capability is that readers retain control.
