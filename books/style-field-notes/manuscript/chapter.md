# Notes from a small observatory

**06:40 — Roof, east side.** The city is already loud at street level, but six floors up the dominant sound is air moving around concrete. Two pigeons occupy the warmest corner of the parapet. They appear to have a standing reservation.

**07:15 — Equipment table.** Coffee, notebook, borrowed binoculars, one pencil sharpened too aggressively. The point of the morning is not serious astronomy. It is to notice what changes when attention is given a schedule.

## What we recorded

1. The color of the sky changed long before the brightness seemed to change.
2. Windows on the western buildings became mirrors one by one.
3. The first visible airplane looked motionless until a cloud passed behind it.
4. Everyone who came through the roof door lowered their voice without being asked.

There is a magazine-like pleasure in observations that do not need to become a thesis. An editorial page can hold fragments, lists, captions, short arguments, and changes of pace without pretending they are chapters in a continuous narrative.

> Not every useful piece of writing wants to become a book-shaped book.

Bookself's Reader can still give such work a coherent visual system. The `editorial` preset uses a humanist sans face and compact rhythm so short entries feel related without being over-formalized.
