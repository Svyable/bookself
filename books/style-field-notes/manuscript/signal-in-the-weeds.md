# Signal in the weeds

*What an abandoned lot can tell a city about heat, water, insects, and the danger of measuring only what has already been paved.*

At 7:12 on a July morning, the vacant parcel behind the old bus depot was already louder than the street. Crickets pulsed from the grass. Goldfinches worked the seed heads. A rabbit disappeared beneath a sheet of corrugated metal that had been there long enough to become habitat.

To a planning map, the lot was blank.

To ecologist Nia Alvarez, it was a sensor array.

## The unofficial station

Alvarez and two graduate students had placed temperature loggers in three kinds of urban ground: asphalt parking lots, managed lawn, and unmanaged lots like this one. The equipment was intentionally modest. Their question was not whether vacant land was beautiful. It was whether the category “vacant” caused planners to ignore ecological work that was already happening.

After six weeks, the pattern was difficult to miss.

| Surface | Midday heat | Water after rain | Visible insect activity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asphalt | highest | rapid runoff | low |
| Managed lawn | moderate | mixed | moderate |
| Unmanaged lot | lower near cover | longer retention | high |

The numbers did not turn weeds into a park. They did make the word *empty* harder to defend.

“Cities are full of places that are only invisible because the spreadsheet does not have a column for them,” Alvarez said.

## A category becomes a budget

Language matters because categories eventually become maintenance schedules, demolition orders, grant criteria, and lines in a capital plan.

An “unused parcel” can be cleared cheaply. A “heat refuge” asks a different question. A “pollinator corridor” suggests continuity across blocks. A “temporary commons” invites people into the definition.

None of those labels is automatically correct. The point is that the first label was not neutral either.

### What the team watches next

The next phase adds observations that resist a single score:

1. soil temperature at different depths;
2. bird and insect diversity across seasons;
3. standing-water duration after heavy rain;
4. informal human use—shortcuts, sitting, play, gardening;
5. what changes after mowing, clearing, or construction.

The work is intentionally longitudinal. A hot week can mislead. A wet spring can flatter. The value appears in patterns that persist after the surprising day has passed.

By 9:30 the lot had lost its morning softness. Traffic thickened along the avenue. The metal siding of the depot brightened in the sun.

Alvarez collected the first logger and brushed seeds from her trousers.

“Blank spaces are rarely blank,” she said. “Usually we have just decided not to read them yet.”
