# The room after rain

The rain had finished before anyone noticed. It left the windows dark and silvered, the street below shining as though somebody had drawn a second city beneath the first. Mara switched on the small lamp beside the chair and the room gathered itself around the circle of light.

She had been trying to read at the table, where the overhead fixture made every page look official. Under the lamp, the same sentences loosened. The book no longer felt like a document that expected something from her. It became a place she could enter.

Outside, a bus sighed at the corner. Water moved along the gutter. Somewhere above her, a cupboard door closed and opened again.

> A room changes when the light stops trying to illuminate everything.

Mara read that line twice, not because it was difficult, but because it seemed to know where it was being read.

The paper-colored page held the words softly. The letters had enough air around them to feel deliberate, and the measure was narrow enough that her eyes could move without hurrying. She forgot that any of this had been chosen.

That was the trick, she thought. Good typesetting did not announce itself as decoration. It arranged the conditions for attention, then disappeared.

She turned the page.

A new paragraph began with the slight inward step of a traditional book. The rhythm felt familiar without feeling old. It reminded her of library copies that had passed through many hands: pages softened at the corners, pencil marks in the margins, a pressed receipt discovered years later as an accidental bookmark.

The lamp warmed the room another degree. Or perhaps the rain had cooled the city enough that warmth could finally be noticed.

Mara kept reading until the buses became less frequent and the reflected city beneath the windows broke apart into ordinary puddles.

When she eventually changed the type size, the book did not object. The page reflowed around her choice and kept its composure. The design had offered a beginning, not a command.

That, too, felt like hospitality.
