# Weather stations

## I. Barometer

The brass needle moves  
without weather in the room.  

Outside, the maple rehearses  
a storm with one leaf.  

My father taps the glass  
as if pressure were a guest  
who might answer the door.  

---

## II. Laundry

Morning hangs from a blue line:  
shirt, sheet, two small socks,  
one red towel giving up the night.  

Wind does not read the labels.  
It takes each thing  
for what it can lift.  

---

## III. Heat advisory

At noon the city lowers its voice.  
Pigeons stand in the fountain rim.  
Buses carry rooms of weather  
from block to block.  

A woman under the pharmacy awning  
opens an umbrella against the sun  
and the sidewalk briefly remembers  
what shade is for.  

---

## IV. Forecast

They say rain by evening,  
which is not the same as rain.  

They say clearing overnight,  
which is not the same as sleep.  

All day we live inside  
these careful futures—  
percentages, arrows, bands of color—  

then at dusk one cold drop  
finds the back of my hand  

and becomes present tense.  

---

## V. Window open

After the storm  
the apartment smells  
of wet brick and leaves.  

Somewhere below  
a spoon strikes a bowl.  
A child laughs once,  
then again because once  
was not enough.  

No instrument records this.  
Still, the room changes.  

I move the chair  
closer to the window.
